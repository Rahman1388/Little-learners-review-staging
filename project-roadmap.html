2. Work remaining / المهام المتبقية

Phase A — Make the current pilot dependable

Confirm actual iPhone Safari sound and taps, test native English MP3 playback, evaluate Arabic device speech limitations, keyboard and VoiceOver accessibility. Fix any reported defects before extending the feature.

Phase B — Expert educational review

Collect Grade 2 teacher feedback, fluent Arabic linguistic approval, child-friendly usability feedback and independently document lesson corrections. No Ministry approval is implied.

Phase C — Expand the curriculum and media

Plan and author grade-specific modules for Grades 1 and 3–6; expand Grade 2 beyond the pilot, add varied original exercises and clearer licensed/reviewed audio, engaging illustrations and optional video-style lessons.

Phase D — Parent-safe learning experience

Design appropriate parent controls, privacy-first progress reporting, accessibility, age-appropriate feedback, and carefully validated certificates. Avoid public child leaderboards or unsupported mastery claims.

Phase E — Brand, legal and mobile release readiness

Confirm a unique bilingual name, conduct trademark/domain checks, approve terms and privacy notices, test security, prepare app packaging, review store rules and publishing costs, and obtain the owner's explicit production-launch authorization.

3. What should be tested next?

Current highest priority: Check actual iPhone Safari playback and tap navigation, followed by independent Grade 2 teacher and fluent Arabic review. This is more important than adding another hundred unreviewed activities.
  1. Open the native sound-check page in Safari and play ring, sing, and hand; note whether each is audible.
  2. Open the Word Family Garden; select a flower, tap the audio controls, complete one Match and Build question, and test Next and Restart.
  3. Ask a Grade 2 teacher and fluent Arabic educator to review the module-by-module checklist. Do not share actual student information.

4. How long until launch?

No reliable fixed date can yet be promised. Finishing the Grade 2 demonstration, building complete curricula for six grades, recording and reviewing bilingual audio, passing child-safety checks and publishing mobile apps are different levels of work. The full Grade 1–6 product is a substantial multi-stage project, not an imminent store release.

We will judge progress by verified milestones and written review sign-offs, not by speculative completion percentages or automatic green badges. Store and external reviewer response times are outside this project's control.
